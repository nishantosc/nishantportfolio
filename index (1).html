Cascade

Founding engineer

I built the last-mile layer of India's national digital healthcare infrastructure, in collaboration with the National Health Authority (NHA).

As a founding engineer, I led the development of compact System-on-Chip (SoC) terminals that delivered full medical desktop environments, even in rural places with unreliable power and connectivity.

The problem

Over 400,000 independent clinics in India had no digital health records. A normal EMR setup needed a ₹20,000+ computer and ₹3,000 to ₹5,000 every month, which small clinics could not afford.

What we built

Lubera, a small plug-in terminal for single-doctor clinics. It connected to any monitor, cost about ₹9,000 (around 60% less than usual setups) and needed no IT person to set up.

How it held up

  • Worked on slow, 2G-level connections.
  • Kept no patient data on the device. Records stayed safe in the cloud.
  • Connected clinics to ABDM, India's national health records system.
  • Handled 60+ patients a day and temperatures up to 45°C in testing.
  • Led to a patent filing for the core technology.
